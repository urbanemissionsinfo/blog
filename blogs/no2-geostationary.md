Attached animation is what I want.
![alt text](blogs/no2-geostationary.gif)

Thanks to [Dan Goldberg](https://www.linkedin.com/in/dan-goldberg-213166103/) -- this animation presents NO2 columnar density from a geostationary satellite over the US, with close to 11 hours of coverage for every location, every day.

What we have (for regions other than the US, EU, China, Korea) are passes.

I want you to understand some numbers on what a "pass" means

A satellite like TROPOMI moves at ~7.5 km per second.

Which means, a location snapshot is under 1-2 seconds.

Coverage from the Southern tip to the Northern tip of India takes ~10 minutes at 1:30 PM local time.

If you search literature, articles using TROPOMI are in 1000's. As monthly averages over all passes, these observations tell a story. There is a lot to learn from these -- emission loads, pollution trends, long-range transport, episodes, etc.

But, (where geostationary is not an option) saying one can use satellite data to tell/predict real-time pollution levels is too much.
